# Tavotto trademark policy

> This document is a project policy, not legal advice.

**Tavotto™** is the name and mark of the Tavotto project. This document explains
what you may do with the name and the logo. It is short on purpose, and it is
not a threat.

## The one thing worth understanding

**An open-source licence covers copyright. It does not cover trademarks.**

Tavotto's [AGPL-3.0-only licence](LICENSE) gives you broad rights to use, study,
modify and redistribute the *software*. It does not give you permission to use
the Tavotto name or logo in a way that suggests your version is the official one,
or that the project endorses it.

These are separate things for a practical reason: the licence is what keeps the
software free, and the mark is what lets people tell where a build came from.
People downloading something called "Tavotto" should be able to rely on it being
the project's own release.

## What you can do without asking

- **Say what your software is.** "Compatible with Tavotto", "Built for Tavotto",
  "Based on Tavotto", "A fork of Tavotto", "Works with Tavotto files" — all fine,
  and no permission is needed. Describing a factual relationship to Tavotto is
  normal and expected.
- **Redistribute unmodified official releases** under their own name.
- **Write about Tavotto.** Articles, tutorials, videos, papers, talks, reviews —
  favourable or not. Screenshots of the interface are fine.
- **Cite it in academic work.**
- **Use the name in a repository, package or account name that clearly reads as
  third-party** — `tavotto-templates`, `awesome-tavotto`, `tavotto-for-julia`.

## What needs care

- **Modified builds.** Distributing a modified Tavotto is explicitly permitted
  by the AGPL — please do. But don't present it as the official Tavotto. Give it
  its own name and say what it is based on. "MyLab Tavotto, based on Tavotto" is
  fine; shipping something modified as plain "Tavotto" is not, because users
  can no longer tell whose build they are running.
- **Anything implying official status.** Don't describe an unofficial build as
  an official release, distribution or product, and don't imply endorsement,
  affiliation or certification that doesn't exist.
- **The logo.** The marks in `assets/brand/` and `assets/icon/` identify official
  Tavotto. Use them unmodified to refer to Tavotto — linking to the project,
  illustrating an article, marking compatibility. Don't use them as the icon of
  your own product, don't modify them into a new logo, and don't use them where
  they would read as a mark of origin for something the project didn't build.
- **Domains, app store listings and social accounts** whose name would read as
  the official project. Ask first.

## What isn't allowed

- Presenting a modified or third-party build as an official Tavotto release.
- Claiming endorsement, partnership, affiliation or certification that doesn't
  exist.
- Registering "Tavotto", or a confusingly similar mark, as your own trademark.
- Using the name or logo for something misleading, or in a way that suggests the
  project stands behind it when it doesn't.

## Forks

Forking is a right the AGPL grants you, and this policy is not intended to
discourage it.

A fork may say — prominently, on its front page, in its README, wherever it
helps — that it is based on, derived from, or a fork of Tavotto. That is a
factual statement about origin and needs no permission.

What a fork should not do is occupy the name: don't call the project itself
"Tavotto", don't publish it as if it were an official release channel, and don't
use the official logo as the fork's own identity. Pick a name of your own and
credit Tavotto in the description. Everyone downstream benefits from being able
to tell the two apart.

## Registration status

**Tavotto is not a registered trademark.** It is used as an unregistered mark
and written as **Tavotto™**.

The **®** symbol is not used anywhere in this project, and must not be, because
it asserts a registration that does not exist. There is no application, serial
number or registration number to cite. See
[`docs/legal/TRADEMARK_REGISTRATION_READINESS.md`](docs/legal/TRADEMARK_REGISTRATION_READINESS.md)
for what would be involved in changing that.
